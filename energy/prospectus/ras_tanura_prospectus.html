Overview

The World's Largest Crude Oil Export Terminal

Ras Tanura is Saudi Aramco's primary crude oil export terminal and one of the largest petroleum export facilities in the world. Located on the Arabian Gulf coast of Eastern Province, Saudi Arabia, it has served as the primary outlet for Saudi crude since first oil export in 1939.

The terminal handles the full range of Saudi crude grades — Arab Extra Light, Arab Light, Arab Medium, Arab Heavy crude; refined products — and connects directly to Abqaiq Plants via pipeline, receiving stabilized crude for loading onto Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCCs) and other tanker classes destined for Asia, Europe, and the Americas.

Abqaiq–Ras Tanura Pipeline Dependency
Ras Tanura sits downstream of Abqaiq Plants in the Saudi Aramco supply chain. Crude processed and stabilized at Abqaiq flows by pipeline to Ras Tanura for export loading. A disruption at either facility cascades to the other — Abqaiq without export capacity, or Ras Tanura without processed crude, each degrades Saudi Arabia's ability to meet export commitments. The two facilities together represent the most concentrated export supply risk in global oil markets.

Supply Chain

Position in Saudi Aramco Export Infrastructure

Ras Tanura is one of four primary Saudi Aramco crude export terminals, alongside Ju'aymah (also on the Arabian Gulf), Yanbu (Red Sea, served by the East-West Petroline), and Jizan. Ras Tanura and Ju'aymah together handle the majority of Saudi crude exports — primarily to Asian markets.

Terminal Coast Primary Markets Bypass Value
Ras Tanura Arabian Gulf China, Japan, South Korea, India Primary Gulf terminal — no internal bypass
Ju'aymah Arabian Gulf Asia Partial Gulf backup to Ras Tanura
Yanbu Red Sea Europe, U.S. East Coast Full Hormuz bypass via Petroline (~5 mmbpd)
Jizan Red Sea Regional Limited — primarily refined products
Gulf Terminal Concentration Risk
Ras Tanura and Ju'aymah both sit on the Arabian Gulf coast and transit through the Strait of Hormuz. A Hormuz closure and a Ras Tanura disruption occurring simultaneously would leave Yanbu — at ~1x lower export capacity — as Saudi Arabia's primary export outlet. The two risks are geographically correlated.
Historical Record

Key Events

1939
First Crude Export. Saudi Arabia's first crude oil export shipment departed Ras Tanura aboard the D.G. Scofield tanker, marking the beginning of what would become the world's largest petroleum export operation. The terminal was developed by the Arabian American Oil Company (Aramco), predecessor to Saudi Aramco.
1973
Arab Oil Embargo. Saudi Arabia participated in the OAPEC oil embargo, sharply curtailing exports through Ras Tanura to the United States and other Western nations supporting Israel in the Yom Kippur War. The embargo ran from October 1973 to March 1974 and triggered the 1973–74 oil crisis, directly leading to the creation of the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve.
1991
Gulf War — Export Disruption Risk. Iraqi Scud missile launches and the broader Gulf War theater put Saudi export infrastructure on heightened alert. Ras Tanura remained operational but the conflict demonstrated the terminal's proximity to active conflict zones in the Persian Gulf region.
2019
Abqaiq-Khurais Attack — Upstream Disruption. The September 14, 2019 drone and missile attack on Abqaiq and Khurais temporarily disrupted Saudi crude processing capacity. Though Ras Tanura itself was not struck, the upstream disruption at Abqaiq directly reduced the volume of processed crude available for export loading at the terminal, demonstrating the pipeline dependency between the two facilities.
2021
Houthi Drone Attack on Ras Tanura. In March 2021, Yemen's Houthi movement launched a drone and missile attack directly targeting Ras Tanura — striking near the oil depot and a residential compound. Saudi Aramco reported no disruption to oil supplies. The attack marked the first direct strike on the terminal itself and elevated the facility's threat profile significantly.
