Overview

The Pipeline Crossroads of North America

Cushing, Oklahoma is the most watched crude oil storage hub in the United States and one of the most strategically significant in the world. Located in Payne County, Oklahoma, it sits at the intersection of approximately 10 inbound and 10 outbound crude oil pipelines connecting producing regions across the mid-continent, Canada, and the Gulf Coast.

Cushing's global significance derives from its role as the designated delivery point for NYMEX West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures contracts — the primary U.S. crude oil benchmark. When WTI futures expire, physical crude must be delivered to or from Cushing. This makes Cushing stock levels the single most watched weekly inventory figure in U.S. energy markets.

Why Cushing Stocks Move Markets
The EIA reports Cushing stocks weekly in the Petroleum Supply Report, released every Wednesday morning. A surprise build (more crude than expected) pressures WTI lower; a surprise draw (less crude than expected) supports WTI higher. Cushing is the physical fulcrum of U.S. crude oil price discovery.

Market Function

WTI Delivery Point & Price Discovery

WTI crude oil futures traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX) specify Cushing as the physical delivery location. At contract expiration, holders of open long positions must accept delivery of crude oil at Cushing; holders of open short positions must make delivery. This physical settlement mechanism anchors WTI futures to real supply and demand conditions at the hub.

The relationship between Cushing inventory levels and WTI price is the most closely watched supply signal in U.S. energy markets. Analysts, traders, and policy makers track the weekly EIA release for evidence of tightening or loosening at the delivery point.

Inventory Condition Market Signal Typical WTI Response
Large surprise build Oversupply at delivery point WTI price pressure downward
Large surprise draw Tightening at delivery point WTI price support upward
Stocks near capacity Storage congestion risk Contango widens; spot WTI may trade at deep discount (cf. April 2020)
Stocks near multi-year low Physical tightness WTI backwardation; spot premium to futures
Historical Record

Key Cushing Stock Events

2011
Cushing Bottleneck — WTI-Brent Spread Widens. Rapid growth in Canadian and mid-continent production flooded Cushing with crude that could not reach Gulf Coast refineries fast enough. Cushing stocks surged, WTI fell to a $25+ discount to Brent — an unprecedented spread. Seaway pipeline was subsequently reversed (2012) to drain Cushing south.
2015
Cushing Near Capacity — U.S. Storage Crisis. U.S. crude production surged under the shale boom while exports remained banned. Cushing stocks approached working capacity limits (~76,000 Thousand Bbl), raising fears of tank tops. WTI traded at deep discounts. Congress subsequently lifted the crude export ban in December 2015.
2020
COVID-19 — WTI Goes Negative. On April 20, 2020, the May WTI futures contract settled at -$37.63/bbl — the first negative crude oil price in history. With Cushing near capacity and demand collapsed due to COVID lockdowns, holders of expiring contracts faced no storage and paid others to take delivery. Physical storage constraints at Cushing were the direct mechanism.
2022
Russia-Ukraine — Cushing Draws to Multi-Year Lows. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, energy demand surged and U.S. crude exports accelerated. Cushing stocks drew steadily to the lowest levels since 2014, contributing to WTI backwardation and elevated prompt prices throughout 2022 and into 2023.
