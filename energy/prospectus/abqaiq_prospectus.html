Executive Summary

Facility Overview

Abqaiq Plants is Saudi Aramco's largest oil processing and crude stabilization facility and, as stated in the Saudi Aramco Annual Report 2025, the largest crude oil stabilization plant in the world. Located in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province, the facility is the primary processing hub for Arabian Extra Light and Arabian Light crude oils — two of the five grades Saudi Aramco produces.

The facility's core function is to receive sour crude oil from upstream gas-oil separation plants (GOSPs) and remove impurities — principally hydrogen sulfide — producing stabilized sweet crude suitable for export and downstream refining. Processed crude is dispatched to Saudi Aramco's domestic refining network and export terminals, including Ras Tanura and Jubail on the east coast and Yanbu on the west coast. Saudi Arabia exported 7.0 million barrels per day of crude oil in 2023, of which Asia received 75%, with China, Japan, South Korea, and India as the top importers.

Saudi Arabia held an estimated 17% of the world's proved oil reserves in 2023 and was the world's top crude oil exporter and OPEC's largest producer that year. Saudi Aramco's stated Maximum Sustainable Capacity is 12.0 million barrels per day, a level the Saudi government directed Aramco to maintain in January 2024. Abqaiq is the central processing node that underpins that capacity.

Oil Processing Unit

  • Receives sour crude from upstream GOSPs
  • Removes hydrogen sulfide and reduces crude volatility
  • Separates oil from associated gases via depressurization
  • Produces Arab Extra Light and Arab Light crude grades

NGL Facility

  • Processes off-gases from the oil processing unit
  • Separates light gases from natural gas liquids product
  • NGL product sent to Ras Tanura refinery for fractionation
  • Light gases transported to other gas plants for processing

Utilities Unit

  • Supplies power, steam, and treated water to oil and NGL units
  • Steam turbine and combustion gas turbine power generation
  • Boiler-based steam generation for processing and compression
  • Reverse osmosis water treatment for boiler makeup supply

Infrastructure

Offtake Routes

According to the Saudi Aramco Annual Report 2025, stabilized crude processed at Abqaiq is dispatched to refining plants at Ras Tanura and Jubail on the east coast and Yanbu on the west coast. Aramco also operates four crude export terminals that contribute to its operational flexibility and supply reliability.

Beyond domestic destinations, Aramco maintains strategic international crude delivery points at Rotterdam (the Netherlands), Sidi Kerir (Egypt), Okinawa and Kiire (Japan), and Ulsan (South Korea) — providing downstream optionality across key consuming regions.

Strategic Analysis

Global Energy Significance

Saudi Arabia was the world's top crude oil exporter and OPEC's largest producer in 2023, exporting 7.0 million barrels per day of crude oil, according to the EIA Country Analysis Brief (October 2024). As the largest crude oil stabilization plant in the world and Saudi Aramco's primary processing facility, Abqaiq is central to sustaining that export capacity. Saudi Aramco reported 99.7% crude oil supply reliability in 2024, reflecting the operational continuity standards applied across its processing infrastructure.

As Saudi Aramco's primary crude stabilization facility, Abqaiq underpins Saudi Arabia's ability to sustain its 12.0 million barrel per day Maximum Sustainable Capacity and remains one of the most strategically important energy facilities in the world.¹³

Historical Reference — September 14, 2019 Attack

A coordinated drone and missile strike on the Abqaiq facility temporarily disrupted Saudi crude processing. The U.S. EIA reported the event in its September 2019 "Today in Energy" publication. Saudi Aramco subsequently restored operational capacity. Specific disruption volumes and price impacts cited in that 2019 EIA report are not reproduced here as current operational data.
