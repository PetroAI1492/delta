Overview

The World's Most Critical Energy Chokepoint

The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, and through it to the Arabian Sea and global shipping lanes. At its narrowest point it spans approximately 21 nautical miles, with two 2-mile-wide shipping lanes — one inbound, one outbound — separated by a 2-mile buffer zone.

Approximately 21 million barrels of crude oil and petroleum products transit Hormuz daily, representing roughly 20% of global petroleum liquids trade. An additional 20% of global liquefied natural gas (LNG) trade passes through the strait, primarily from Qatar. There is no viable alternative route for the volume of energy that transits Hormuz — it is, by any measure, the single most consequential energy chokepoint on earth.

No Bypass at Scale
The primary alternatives — the Abqaiq-Yanbu pipeline (Saudi Arabia, ~5 mmbpd capacity) and the Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (~1.5 mmbpd) — together cover less than a third of normal Hormuz throughput. A full closure would remove approximately 15–17 million b/d from global markets with no substitute route.

Historical Record

Major Hormuz Disruption Events

1980–88
Tanker War — Iran-Iraq War. Both sides attacked oil tankers transiting the Persian Gulf. The U.S. Navy conducted Operation Earnest Will (1987–88), escorting Kuwaiti tankers reflagged under the U.S. flag. Approximately 451 ships were attacked during the conflict. Hormuz remained open but at elevated risk throughout.
1988
Operation Praying Mantis. U.S. Navy struck Iranian naval vessels and oil platforms in the Gulf in retaliation for Iran mining international waters. The largest U.S. naval surface engagement since World War II. Strait remained open.
2019
Tanker Attacks & Mine Incidents. Multiple commercial tankers attacked or mined in the Gulf of Oman near the strait entrance. U.S. attributed attacks to Iran. Insurance premiums for Hormuz transit spiked. No closure occurred but transit risk elevated sharply.
2019
UK Tanker Seizure. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) seized the British-flagged tanker Stena Impero in the Strait of Hormuz in July 2019, holding it for two months. Direct response to UK seizure of Iranian tanker Grace 1 in Gibraltar.
2023–24
Houthi Red Sea Campaign — Spillover Risk. While Houthi attacks targeted Red Sea / Bab-el-Mandeb shipping rather than Hormuz directly, the campaign demonstrated the capacity of Iran-aligned proxies to disrupt major chokepoints. Insurance and routing costs for Gulf exporters rose. Hormuz itself remained unaffected.
Alternatives

Bypass Pipeline Capacity

Two overland pipelines offer partial bypass of Hormuz. Neither approaches normal strait throughput.

Pipeline Country Capacity Destination Notes
East-West Pipeline (Petroline) Saudi Arabia ~5.0 mmbpd Yanbu, Red Sea Abqaiq to Yanbu; primary Saudi bypass route
Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (ADCOP) UAE ~1.5 mmbpd Fujairah, Gulf of Oman Habshan to Fujairah; bypasses strait entirely
Combined bypass capacity — ~6.5 mmbpd — vs ~21 mmbpd normal Hormuz flow — ~31% coverage
