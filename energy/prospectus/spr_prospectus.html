Overview

What Is the Strategic Petroleum Reserve?

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) is the world's largest emergency crude oil stockpile, authorized by the Energy Policy and Conservation Act of 1975 following the 1973–74 Arab oil embargo. Operated by the U.S. Department of Energy, the SPR stores crude oil in a network of underground salt caverns along the Gulf Coast of Texas and Louisiana, providing a buffer against supply disruptions that could threaten national economic and security interests.

The International Energy Agency (IEA) requires member countries to hold emergency petroleum reserves equivalent to at least 90 days of net imports. The SPR is the primary U.S. mechanism for meeting this obligation.

Strategic Context
The SPR is not a price stabilization tool — it is an emergency supply mechanism. Statutory authority for releases requires a Presidential Finding of a "severe energy supply interruption" or a coordinated IEA release. Congress has authorized non-emergency sales on multiple occasions to raise revenue or fund other programs, reducing the reserve's cushion over time.
Infrastructure

Salt Cavern Storage Sites

Crude oil is stored in solution-mined caverns in naturally occurring salt domes — an ideal medium due to salt's impermeability, self-sealing properties, and structural stability. Each site consists of multiple individual caverns.

Historical Record

Major SPR Drawdown Events

The SPR has been drawn down in response to supply emergencies, coordinated IEA releases, and congressionally mandated sales. The following events are sourced from DOE and EIA public records.

1991
Gulf War — Operation Desert Storm. 33.75 million barrels released as part of an IEA coordinated action following Iraq's invasion of Kuwait and the resulting disruption to Middle East crude supplies.
2005
Hurricanes Katrina and Rita. 30.6 million barrels released (11 million swapped) after Gulf Coast refinery and pipeline infrastructure was severely damaged, interrupting domestic fuel supply.
2011
Libya Supply Disruption — IEA Coordinated Release. 30.64 million barrels released (U.S. share) as part of an IEA collective action responding to the loss of Libyan crude exports during the civil war.
2020
COVID-19 — Congressional Mandated Sales. Multiple congressionally authorized sales totaling approximately 10 million barrels during the pandemic period to fund federal programs.
2022
Russia-Ukraine War — Record Emergency Release. 180 million barrels released over six months (approximately 1 million b/d) — the largest drawdown in SPR history — in response to energy market disruptions following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. SPR fell to lowest levels since 1984.
