0.  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Maritime flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain stable, with ~20–21 MBPD crude and condensate transits, accounting for ~20% of globally traded petroleum. Henry Hub has surged by $3.46 to $7.72/MMBtu, driven by seasonal demand rather than Gulf maritime flows. Brent is currently priced at $69.77/bbl, with a widening spread to WTI at $7.24/bbl, reflecting geopolitical risk premiums. US nat gas storage has decreased by 144 Bcf, signaling tightening supply conditions. Risk bias: Stable operational posture; asymmetric upside disruption risk.

1.  STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint, facilitating the transit of ~20–21 MBPD crude and condensate, which represents ~20% of globally traded petroleum. Key producers such as Saudi Arabia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, and Iran rely heavily on this route for their exports, with Saudi Arabia alone exporting around 7 MBPD. The US maintains a robust production posture at 13,735 MBPD, with exports recently increasing to 4,590 MBPD. Regional geopolitical volatility preserves asymmetric disruption risk.

2.  OPERATIONAL ENVIRONMENT

The operational environment remains stable, with US 5th Fleet and Coalition Maritime Forces maintaining a vigilant presence in the region. The IRGCN presence sustains latent escalation potential, particularly as tensions fluctuate. Current force levels indicate readiness to respond to any emerging threats in the Strait.

3.  COMMERCIAL SHIPPING & ENERGY MARKET INDICATORS

The most significant market movement is the Henry Hub price, which has increased by $3.46 to $7.72/MMBtu, driven by seasonal demand factors and not directly linked to Gulf maritime flows. Brent crude is currently priced at $69.77/bbl, down $1.04 from the previous week, while WTI is at $62.53/bbl, resulting in a widening Brent-WTI spread of $7.24/bbl, reflecting elevated geopolitical risk premiums.

4.  72-HOUR WATCHPOINTS

Key watchpoints include Brent breaking above approximately $73.26/bbl (+5% from current levels), which would signal increased disruption risk. Additionally, Henry Hub exceeding $8.50/MMBtu would confirm a supply crunch beyond seasonal norms. A geopolitical trigger such as an IRGCN vessel seizure or a closure of the TSS lane would also heighten disruption risk.
