0.  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

No confirmed disruption to maritime flows as of 22 February 2026 | 21:43 EST. • Henry Hub natural gas prices increased by $3.46 to $7.72/MMBtu, reflecting seasonal demand pressures. • Brent crude is priced at 69.77 USD/bbl, with a widening Brent-WTI spread at $7.24/bbl. • US natural gas storage levels decreased to 2,070 Bcf, indicating tightening supply. • Risk bias: elevated due to ongoing geopolitical tensions in the region.

1.  STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint, with approximately ~20–21 MBPD crude and condensate transiting, accounting for roughly 20% of globally traded petroleum. Key Gulf producers, including Saudi Arabia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, and Iran, exhibit significant export dependency, with Saudi Arabia alone relying on oil exports for over 90% of its revenue. The US maintains a robust production posture at 13,735 MBPD, with exports reaching 4,590 MBPD, partially offsetting regional supply risks. Regional geopolitical volatility preserves asymmetric disruption risk.

2.  OPERATIONAL ENVIRONMENT

The operational environment remains stable, with no immediate threats to maritime operations. The US 5th Fleet continues to monitor the Strait of Hormuz, while the IRGC Navy maintains a presence in the area, underscoring the potential for rapid escalation. Coalition Maritime Forces (CMF) are also actively engaged in ensuring safe passage for commercial shipping.

3.  COMMERCIAL SHIPPING & ENERGY MARKET INDICATORS

The most significant market movement is the Henry Hub natural gas price, which has surged to $7.72/MMBtu, driven by seasonal demand but not directly linked to Gulf maritime flows. Brent crude is currently priced at 69.77 USD/bbl, reflecting a $1.04 decline since mid-February, while the Brent-WTI spread has widened to $7.24/bbl, indicating an elevated geopolitical risk premium. WTI crude is trading at 62.53 USD/bbl, with overall market sentiment cautious amid fluctuating supply dynamics.

4.  72-HOUR WATCHPOINTS

Watchpoints include Brent breaking above approximately 73.26 USD/bbl (+5% from current levels), which would signal increased market tension. Additionally, Henry Hub exceeding 8.50 USD/MMBtu would confirm a supply crunch beyond seasonal norms, indicating potential disruptions. A significant geopolitical trigger to monitor is any IRGCN vessel seizure or closure of the TSS lane, which would heighten disruption risks in the Strait.
