0.  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Maritime flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain stable with ~20–21 MBPD crude and condensate transits. The most notable data move is Henry Hub nat gas rising to $7.72/MMBtu, up $3.46 since December 2025. Brent crude is priced at $69.77/bbl, with a widening Brent-WTI spread at $7.24/bbl. US nat gas storage has decreased to 2,070 Bcf, signaling potential supply tightening. Risk bias: Stable operational posture; asymmetric upside disruption risk.

1.  STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

The Strait of Hormuz is critical, facilitating ~20–21 MBPD crude and condensate transits, accounting for ~20% of globally traded petroleum. Key Gulf producers, including Saudi Arabia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, and Iran, heavily rely on this passage for their export strategies. Current US production stands at 13,735 MBPD, with exports at 4,590 MBPD, underscoring the strategic importance of the region. Regional geopolitical volatility preserves asymmetric disruption risk.

2.  OPERATIONAL ENVIRONMENT

The operational environment remains stable, supported by the US 5th Fleet's presence and ongoing maritime security initiatives. However, IRGCN presence sustains latent escalation potential, particularly as tensions persist in the region. The current force posture indicates readiness to respond to any emerging threats in the Strait of Hormuz.

3.  COMMERCIAL SHIPPING & ENERGY MARKET INDICATORS

The most significant market movement is the Henry Hub nat gas price, which has risen to $7.72/MMBtu, reflecting seasonal demand but not directly linked to Gulf maritime flows. Brent crude is currently priced at $69.77/bbl, with a Brent-WTI spread of $7.24/bbl, indicating elevated geopolitical risk premiums. WTI is at $62.53/bbl, showing a slight decline, while heating oil remains stable at $2.29/gal.

4.  72-HOUR WATCHPOINTS

Watchpoints include Brent breaking above approximately $73.26/bbl (5% increase from $69.77/bbl), which would signal heightened market tension. Additionally, a Henry Hub price exceeding $8.50/MMBtu would confirm a supply crunch beyond seasonal norms. Finally, any IRGCN vessel seizure or closure of transit security lanes would escalate disruption risk significantly.
