What makes this offering unusual
Five features unlike prior large IPOs
◆
Fixed price, no range. S-1 / Reuters Jun 3 Standard IPOs set a range and adjust based on institutional demand. SpaceX set $135 as a final price — not a floor, not a ceiling.
◆
30% retail allocation. Reuters Jun 3 Typical large IPOs reserve 5–10% for retail investors. SpaceX is allocating approximately 30% through Fidelity, Schwab, Robinhood, SoFi, and Morgan Stanley E*Trade.
◆
~3% initial float. S-1; WEEX analysis 556.6 million shares are being offered of approximately 13 billion total shares outstanding. A small float against large demand can cause significant first-day price volatility.
◆
Friends and family: no lockup. S-1 prospectus Musk and top executives face approximately one year lockup. Shares distributed to friends and family are not subject to any lockup restriction and may be sold on day one.
◆
$2,000 minimum. Fidelity, Jun 4 2026 An unusually low barrier for retail participation in an offering of this scale. Designed for broad retail access, including international investors.

What SpaceX actually is — Source: SpaceX S-1
Two revenue streams
Starlink — Satellite Internet ~58% of revenue
43M+ subscribers as of the S-1 filing. One of the world's largest satellite broadband networks. The primary driver of SpaceX's growth narrative and a central component of the $1.77T valuation. S-1, May 2026
Launch Services — Falcon 9 / Starship ~42% of revenue
Handles the majority of global orbital launches. Falcon 9 is the proven high-cadence workhorse. Starship is the next-generation heavy-lift system — still in development, not yet revenue-generating at scale.
xAI (Musk's AI company) was merged into SpaceX in February 2026. AI losses of approximately $2.5B per quarter are now consolidated on SpaceX's income statement. S-1

Six risks every investor should understand
Structural risks
HIGH
Valuation — 96× price-to-sales S-1 / public filings
At $135/share, SpaceX is priced for substantial future growth. The 96× price-to-sales ratio is among the highest for any company at IPO at this scale. Among all companies currently valued above $1T, none generates less than $58B in annual revenue. SpaceX generates $18.7B. The valuation is pricing a future state, not the current business.
HIGH
Musk controls 85% of votes S-1
Class A shareholders (public investors) hold minimal governance rights. SpaceX qualifies as a "controlled company" under Nasdaq listing rules, which exempts it from certain standard corporate governance requirements. Musk can direct major strategic decisions without shareholder approval.
HIGH
Tesla — what we know vs what is speculation
FACT
Tesla is mentioned 87 times in the SpaceX S-1. The filing grants SpaceX authority to pursue future acquisitions. SpaceX and Tesla already have documented financial relationships including $697M in Megapack and Cybertruck purchases. S-1
ANALYST OPINION
Wedbush analyst Dan Ives has publicly stated an 80–90% merger probability within a year of the IPO. This is one analyst's view and not confirmed by either company. Wedbush, May 2026
UNKNOWN
Whether management intends to pursue a Tesla acquisition, on what timeline, at what price, and on what terms for SpaceX public shareholders. Readers should review the S-1 risk factors and capital structure sections directly for disclosed governance provisions.
Operational risks
HIGH
Persistent and accelerating losses S-1
FY2025 net loss: $4.94B. Q1 2026 net loss: $4.28B — annualizing at approximately $17B per year. SpaceX has not reported a profitable fiscal year in its history. The xAI integration is contributing approximately $2.5B per quarter in AI-related losses.
HIGH
Tiny float — day-one price risk S-1 / WEEX
Approximately 3% of total shares are being offered publicly (556.6M of ~13B). Investors who cannot obtain IPO allocation at $135 and buy at market open on June 12 may pay a significant premium. Friends and family share recipients face no lockup and can sell immediately at open.
MED
Regulatory, spectrum, and launch risk S-1 risk factors
Starship development requires continued FAA launch approvals. Starlink operations depend on spectrum rights and regulatory approvals in dozens of international markets. These risks are disclosed in the S-1 risk factors section and should be read directly.

Questions to ask before deciding
Due diligence checklist
→
Signing a Client Certification under FINRA Rule 5130 confirms your eligibility to receive IPO shares — it does not obligate you to purchase. You may decline any allocation offered.
→
Ask your broker: what is my allocation size, is there a lockup on my shares, and what is the minimum commitment amount?
→
If you decline IPO allocation, SPCX will trade on the open market on June 12. The opening price may differ significantly from $135 due to the small float and expected demand imbalance.
→
Anti-flipping rules vary by broker. At Fidelity, selling within 15 days of the IPO may restrict participation in future IPO offerings through that platform. Fidelity, Jun 4
→
Read the S-1 risk factors directly — particularly the sections on "controlled company" status, the Tesla cross-references, and the xAI loss disclosures. The S-1 is publicly available at SEC EDGAR.
→
The underwriting banks publishing positive research and price targets are being compensated for selling this deal. Independent research — including analysts with no role in the offering — should be weighted more heavily.
Questions worth discussing with your advisor
The right question is not "will SpaceX succeed?" — it may well succeed at some level. The right questions are: "at what price does that success justify this valuation?" and "what happens to my position if a Tesla transaction occurs at terms unfavorable to SpaceX public shareholders?"

Morningstar's independent fair value model estimates $63/share — 54% below the IPO price. This is one model, built on specific assumptions, and other analysts will reach different conclusions. It is included here because Morningstar has no financial stake in this offering. The gap between $63 and $135 represents the premium being charged for Starlink's projected growth, Starship's long-term potential, and Musk's execution track record — none of which are guaranteed.

A disciplined approach: understand the specific terms of your allocation before committing, seek out independent research alongside underwriter analysis, and consider waiting for quarterly financial filings after the IPO before making a larger capital commitment. The stock will be available after June 12. Missing the first day of trading is not the same as missing the investment.
