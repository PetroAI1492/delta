Strategic Overview

PRC Cyber Operations — Doctrine and Intent

The People's Republic of China operates the world's most sophisticated state-sponsored cyber program, encompassing espionage, intellectual property theft, and — most critically — pre-positioning within U.S. critical infrastructure for potential future disruption. Unlike financially motivated cybercriminals or ransomware operators, PRC-sponsored actors often establish persistent access years in advance of any operational use, consistent with a military pre-positioning doctrine rather than immediate exploitation.

The February 2024 CISA advisory confirmed that Volt Typhoon actors had maintained access to some U.S. critical infrastructure networks for five or more years without detection. The stated intent, assessed by U.S. intelligence, is to pre-position for disruption of logistics, energy, water, and communications in the event of a military conflict — particularly a Taiwan Strait contingency.

Strategic Assessment — CISA / FBI / NSA
PRC cyber actors are not primarily seeking to steal data from critical infrastructure — they are seeking to disrupt or destroy it at a time of their choosing. This is a fundamentally different threat model than espionage. Dwell times of 5+ years indicate pre-positioning, not opportunistic access. The energy sector is a primary target. [1][2]

Tactics, Techniques & Procedures

How They Operate

Living Off the Land (LOTL) — The Defining Technique
Volt Typhoon's signature technique uses built-in Windows tools (wmic, ntdsutil, netsh, PowerShell) and legitimate credentials rather than custom malware. This makes detection extremely difficult — behavioral anomalies rather than malware signatures are the primary detection vector. Traditional antivirus and signature-based defenses are largely ineffective against LOTL techniques. [1]

Incident History

Key Events — 2021 to Present

2021
Microsoft Exchange — Hafnium / APT40. PRC-affiliated actors exploited four zero-day vulnerabilities in Microsoft Exchange Server, compromising over 250,000 servers globally within days of disclosure. Targets included defense contractors, law firms, and infectious disease researchers. CISA Emergency Directive 21-02 issued. [4]
2023
Volt Typhoon — Critical Infrastructure Pre-positioning Disclosed. Microsoft and CISA disclosed that Volt Typhoon had maintained persistent access to U.S. critical infrastructure networks — including energy, water, transportation, and communications — for up to five years. Focus assessed as pre-positioning for disruption rather than data theft. Five Eyes joint advisory issued. [1][2]
2024
Salt Typhoon — U.S. Telecom Compromise. Salt Typhoon compromised at least nine major U.S. telecommunications carriers, including AT&T and Verizon, obtaining access to CALEA lawful intercept systems used by law enforcement. Senior government officials' communications were reportedly accessed. Senate Intelligence Committee briefed. [3]
2024
CISA Emergency Directive — Ivanti VPN Exploitation. CISA issued Emergency Directive 24-01 after PRC-linked actors exploited zero-days in Ivanti Connect Secure VPN appliances, targeting federal agencies and critical infrastructure operators. Volt Typhoon among actors attributed. [2]
2024
APT40 — Five Eyes Joint Advisory. Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, New Zealand, South Korea, UK, and U.S. issued a joint advisory attributing APT40 to China's MSS Hainan State Security Department. Advisory detailed APT40's rapid exploitation of newly disclosed CVEs — sometimes within hours of public disclosure — across maritime, defense, and government targets globally. [4]
2025
Treasury Department Breach — Silk Typhoon. PRC state-sponsored actors (Silk Typhoon / APT41 affiliate) breached the U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and Office of the Secretary — the offices responsible for administering China-related sanctions. Access obtained via compromised BeyondTrust remote support software. [5]

Key Mitigations — CISA Guidance

The following mitigations are drawn from CISA/FBI/NSA joint advisories. They are specifically tailored to counter LOTL techniques used by Volt Typhoon and related PRC actors.

Priority Mitigation Addresses
1 — Critical Enforce phishing-resistant MFA on all remote access and privileged accounts Credential-based initial access used by Volt Typhoon
2 — Critical Patch Fortinet, Ivanti, Cisco, and Citrix appliances within 48 hours of critical CVE disclosure APT40 and Volt Typhoon perimeter exploitation
3 — Critical Audit and restrict SOHO router access to OT/ICS networks; replace end-of-life devices Volt Typhoon SOHO router proxy infrastructure
4 — High Deploy behavioral analytics (EDR/XDR) tuned to detect LOTL tool abuse Living-off-the-land detection gap
