Clinical Trial Navigation Resource

This project was created to help colorectal cancer patients and caregivers more easily understand what clinical trials may currently be available.

The goal is simple: make publicly available clinical trial information easier to navigate and easier to understand for people going through treatment.

Reports are generated from ClinicalTrials.gov and are offered free to patient advocacy organizations, patient navigators, and support groups.

Why I Built It

In 2024 I was diagnosed with Stage IIA colon cancer.

During treatment I repeatedly asked a simple question: Are there clinical trials open for someone like me right now?

The information existed on ClinicalTrials.gov, but it was difficult for patients and caregivers to quickly understand which trials might apply to their situation.

In late February 2026 — about a week after the idea occurred to me — I built a pipeline that tracks open colorectal cancer trials and produces a structured report designed for patients, caregivers, and patient navigators.

The system polls ClinicalTrials.gov twice daily, classifies trials by stage, biomarker target, and phase, and generates a report containing:

  • A structured overview of open trials
  • A clinical summary
  • A plain-language appendix for patients and caregivers
  • Consistent, rule-based classification of trial attributes
  • AI-generated summaries based strictly on U.S. government registry data

All trial data originates from the U.S. government registry at ClinicalTrials.gov. The system does not alter registry data; it only organizes and summarizes it.
