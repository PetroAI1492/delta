In 2024 I was diagnosed with Stage IIA colon cancer. During treatment I kept asking: are there clinical trials open for someone like me right now? The information existed on ClinicalTrials.gov but wasn't easy to act on. In late February 2026 — about a week after the idea hit me — I built something I wished I'd had during treatment.

This report is generated automatically twice daily from U.S. government registry data. Offered free to patient advocacy organizations, patient navigators, and support groups.

How It Works

The system ingests the ClinicalTrials.gov RSS feed twice daily, classifies each new trial deterministically by stage, biomarker target, and therapy class, scores trials by scientific significance, and generates a structured PDF report. Summaries are AI-generated from trial titles only — no inference, no speculation, no outside knowledge introduced.
⇣ Ingest
ClinicalTrials.gov RSS feed · parsed twice daily · duplicate detection by URL · stored in SQLite with published date, description, and metadata
▶ Classify
Deterministic keyword matching against title + description · stage (I–IV) · biomarker target (KRAS, BRAF, HER2, MSI, VEGF, EGFR) · therapy class (immunotherapy, cell therapy, new trial) · phase · breakthrough score
📄 Report
Structured PDF generated via HTML + Edge headless · three distinct output sections · breakthrough score badges surface highest-signal trials · updated on every run

Report Sections

Clinical Summary
AI-generated analytical paragraph for oncologists and researchers. Identifies trial types, patient populations, and biomarker themes present in the dataset. Grounded strictly in registry data — no speculation or invented claims.
Data Intelligence Snapshot
Structured metrics block: trial count, Stage IV breakdown, biomarker-targeted trials, pMMR/MSS focus, cell therapy activity, phase distribution, and breakthrough signal count. Designed for data professionals.
Patient-Friendly Summary
Plain-language appendix written for patients, caregivers, and navigators. Names specific trials and drugs. Explains biomarker terms in plain English. Tells patients what to ask their oncologist — never what decision to make.

⚡ Trials to Watch

Each trial is scored for scientific significance based on biomarker target, therapy class, disease stage, and trial phase. Trials scoring above a threshold — or targeting key biomarkers (KRAS, BRAF, HER2, MSI, PD-1/PD-L1) — are surfaced in a dedicated callout section above the full event timeline, with scores shown inline on each event card.
