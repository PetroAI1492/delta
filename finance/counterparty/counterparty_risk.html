Counterparty risk screening answers the question every compliance officer, banker, and corporate treasurer must ask before transacting: who am I doing business with? Three public regulatory lists together define the universe of known-bad or high-risk counterparties: OFAC identifies sanctioned entities, FATF identifies high-risk jurisdictions, and the BIS Entity List identifies export-controlled parties.

OFAC 50% Rule — Critical for Beneficial Ownership
An entity owned 50% or more by an SDN-listed party is itself blocked under OFAC regulations — even if that entity does not appear on the SDN list. Example: SDN-listed Party A owns 51% of Entity X → Entity X is blocked even if not listed. This means screening only the SDN list is insufficient. Beneficial ownership must be traced to identify indirect SDN exposure. This is where the question "who am I doing business with?" becomes structurally complex. [1]

BIS vs OFAC — Different Triggers, Same Question
OFAC prohibits transactions. BIS restricts exports. A counterparty can be on the BIS Entity List but not the OFAC SDN — meaning you can transact financially but cannot export controlled technology to them. Both lists must be screened independently. China dominates the BIS Entity List due to military-civil fusion concerns; Russia dominates OFAC. A full counterparty risk picture requires both. [2]
